1. Principal component analysis

Instructor’s NoteThe gap is the reference scale

If the top two directions explain almost the same variance, a small perturbation can choose a very different winner. With a clear gap, the winning direction is stable. The sign is only a label for that direction: reversing an arrow does not change its axis.

3.2. Phase transition phenomenon in community detection*

◇ Low priority — not examined: Phase transition phenomenon in community detection

In fact, our spectral algorithm works in a much more challenging (sparser) regime with stronger results. We say that an estimator \(z\)zz achieves exact recovery if \[\PR\{z=z^*\ \text{or}\ z=-z^*\}=1-o(1),\qquad n\to\infty.\]ℙ{z=z*orz=−z*}=1−o(1),n→∞.\mathbb{P}\{z=z^*\ \text{or}\ z=-z^*\}=1-o(1),\qquad n\to\infty.   • Coordinate accuracy. Even an improved operator-norm bound alone does not establish exact recovery: one must also control individual eigenvector coordinates so every sign is correct.

  • Reference and scope. This is the additional step in [1] (Theorem 3.2). The boundary case \(\sqrt a-\sqrt b=\sqrt2\)a−b=2\sqrt a-\sqrt b=\sqrt2 is not asserted here.

Comparison with the MLE.

In an earlier lecture, we derived the MLE for estimating \(z^*\)z*z^*, which involves a complicated optimization problem. In comparison, the spectral method is fast, with an \(O(n^2)\)O(n2)O(n^2) cost per dense matrix-vector iteration. Computing a full eigendecomposition generally costs \(O(n^3)\)O(n3)O(n^3); the iteration count and accuracy matter.

For exact recovery, the MLE cannot do better than the spectral method in our setting. It cannot break the information limit \(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b} \sim \sqrt{2}\)a−b∼2\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b} \sim \sqrt{2} in Theorem 6.
