Why this part matters

This optional part studies how shrinkage and sparsity can improve estimation in many dimensions. The motivating contrast is the Normal mean: optimality among unbiased estimators does not prevent a biased rule from improving squared-error risk in dimension at least three.

The lectures connect James–Stein shrinkage, coordinatewise thresholding and sparse recovery. Earlier ideas return: bias–variance comparisons, Gaussian maxima, soft thresholding and convex optimization.

Optional enrichment. All three lectures in this part are optional and are not examined. Their high, mid and low labels organize a reading path within the optional material.

Learning objectives

  1. Explain how shrinkage can improve risk despite introducing bias (Course outcome 3).

  2. Relate Gaussian tail scales to coordinate thresholds (Course outcome 4).

  3. Connect sparsity with thresholding and convex estimation (Course outcome 5).
