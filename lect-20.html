1.1. Restricted null space property

◆ High priorityRestricted null space property

We ask the question: What does the solution \(\argmin\{\|\theta\|_1: \theta \in \theta^* + \text{Ker}(X)\}\)arg min{∥θ∥1:θ∈θ*+Ker(X)}\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\limits\{\|\theta\|_1: \theta \in \theta^* + \text{Ker}(X)\} look like?

  • Expand the ball. Let \(B_1(r)=\{\theta:\|\theta\|_1\le r\}\)B1(r)={θ:∥θ∥1≤r}B_1(r)=\{\theta:\|\theta\|_1\le r\}. Increase \(r\)rr from zero until this ball first intersects the feasible affine subspace.

  • Optimal value. The first contact radius is the minimum objective value.

  • Uniqueness. A single contact point gives a unique optimizer; contact along a face gives multiple optimizers.

  • Convexity. The \(\ell_1\)ℓ1\ell_1 norm is convex, not strictly convex: the midpoint of two equal-norm points can have the same norm. Thus the number of intersections with a level surface alone does not determine whether the prescribed \(\theta^*\)θ*\theta^* is recovered.

Blessing of high dimensions.

The \(\ell_1\)ℓ1\ell_1 ball in \(p\)pp dimensions, namely \[\begin{aligned} B_1^p(r) = \{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^p: \|\theta\|_1 \le r\} \end{aligned}\]B1p(r)={θ∈ℝp:∥θ∥1≤r}\begin{aligned} B_1^p(r) = \{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^p: \|\theta\|_1 \le r\} \end{aligned} is intuitively very pointy, meaning that the “angle” is very sharp at all corner points. This is in contrast with low dimensions such as 2D. More concretely, consider the simple case \(\theta^* = (1,0,0,\ldots,0)^\top\)θ*=(1,0,0,…,0)⊤\theta^* = (1,0,0,\ldots,0)^\top. BP recovers this sparse signal \(\theta^*\)θ*\theta^* iff the feasible affine subspace first touches the \(\ell_1\)ℓ1\ell_1 ball at \(\theta^*\)θ*\theta^* alone, i.e., \[B_1(\|\theta^*\|_1) \bigcap \big(\theta^* + \text{Ker}(X)\big) = \{ \theta^* \}.\]B1(∥θ*∥1)⋂(θ*+Ker(X))={θ*}.B_1(\|\theta^*\|_1) \bigcap \big(\theta^* + \text{Ker}(X)\big) = \{ \theta^* \}.

Instructor’s NotePointy corners and enough measurements

Keep the geometric picture: the \(\ell_1\)ℓ1\ell_1 ball has corners on coordinate axes, so a feasible set can touch a sparse point first. Each measurement removes a direction of freedom. Recovery succeeds when the remaining null directions cannot move us away from the sparse corner without increasing the \(\ell_1\)ℓ1\ell_1 norm.

  • Recovery criterion. For one Gaussian measurement, \(X=a^\top\in\R^{1\times p}\)X=a⊤∈ℝ1×pX=a^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times p}, and target \(e_1\)e1e_1, basis pursuit recovers \(e_1\)e1e_1 uniquely exactly when \(|a_1|>\max_{j>1}|a_j|\)|a1|>maxj>1|aj||a_1|>\max_{j>1}|a_j|.

  • Why. Indeed the cheapest representation of \(y=a_1\)y=a1y=a_1 uses the column with largest absolute value.

  • Success probability. The continuous i.i.d. entries are exchangeable, so this success probability is \(1/p\)1/p1/p.

  • Role of more measurements. One measurement leaves too much freedom; the bounds below quantify how additional measurements improve recovery.

  • The tuning parameter must be calibrated to the noise correlations under the stated design normalization.
