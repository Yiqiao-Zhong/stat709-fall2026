About the course

STAT 709 is a core course in the Statistics Ph.D. curriculum and the first course in the mathematical statistics sequence, followed by STAT 710. It introduces the foundations of statistical theory and prepares doctoral students to read, evaluate, and conduct theoretical statistical research.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

  • Formulate statistical estimation problems using rigorous mathematical language.
  • Derive fundamental properties of estimators, including bias and variance.
  • Compare estimators and explain the strengths and limitations of competing methods.
  • Apply theoretical tools, including concentration inequalities, to new statistical problems and present the resulting arguments as mathematical proofs.
  • Distinguish classical large-sample theory from high-dimensional statistical theory and critically evaluate recent research.

Homework

Homework 1 — Assignment PDF

Weekly assignments provide practice with the definitions, calculations, and proofs developed in class. Problems will come from several sources, including Jun Shao’s Mathematical Statistics, second edition.

  • Posting and deadlines. Homework will normally be posted by Sunday evening and due approximately one week later. Consult Canvas for each assignment’s exact deadline.
  • Submission. Submit all work through Canvas. LaTeX and legible handwritten solutions are both acceptable.

Late submissions

A 20% deduction applies to work submitted after the deadline but before solutions are posted. Work submitted after solutions are posted receives no credit.
