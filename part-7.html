Part VII

Testing and confidence sets

How do we turn estimates into decisions and uncertainty statements?

Why this part matters

Most of the contents of the Part VII lectures are taken from Bickel and Doksum (BD), Mathematical Statistics: Basic Ideas and Selected Topics, Volume I, second edition, and adapted to the course notation. These are newly prepared lectures, not the original lecture notes from previous years.

This part connects estimates to testing decisions and uncertainty statements. Lecture 21 develops hypothesis tests, \(p\)pp-values, and likelihood-ratio methods, starting with simple hypotheses and then moving to composite hypotheses. Lecture 22 will develop confidence sets and their connection to tests.

Roadmap

  • Lecture 21: Hypothesis tests and \(p\)pp-values. HTML notes Student PDF. Errors, level and power; a Normal example; \(p\)pp-values; Neyman–Pearson; composite hypotheses and generalized likelihood ratios.

  • Lecture 22: Confidence sets (planned). Coverage, pivots, test inversion and approximate intervals.

What you need from earlier lectures

  • Tests as decisions

  • Likelihood

  • CLT

  • Delta method

  • Fisher information

Where this leads

Testing a candidate parameter value and reporting a confidence set are closely related: collect the values that the corresponding tests do not reject. Lecture 22 will make coverage and this duality precise.

Readings

Lecture 21 is based primarily on BD Sections 4.1–4.3, with additional credited explanations identified in the lecture references. The complete Neyman–Pearson proof is optional reading.

Checkpoint status

For the available material, use Lecture 21’s Key ideas and supplemental reference and practice with complete solutions. A completed-part checkpoint will accompany the confidence-set lecture.
