● Mid priorityFrom consistency to uncertainty

Overview.

  • Question. Lecture 9 asked whether an estimator approaches its population target. This lecture asks what its remaining error looks like after we put it on a useful scale.

  • Tools. We introduce convergence in distribution, state the central limit theorem (CLT), and study three ways to understand its Normal limit: characteristic functions, moments, and Lindeberg’s replacement argument.

  • Proof routes. The first gives our complete proof under finite second moments; the other two expose useful ideas for further work.

Quantifying uncertainty of estimation.

  • Consistency. Consistency says that \(\widehat\theta_n\)θ̂n\widehat\theta_n approaches the true parameter \(\theta\)θ\theta as \(n\to\infty\)n→∞n\to\infty. But how is \(\widehat\theta_n\)θ̂n\widehat\theta_n distributed at a large sample size?

  • Repeated experiments. Imagine repeating a scientific experiment, or changing the random seed in a computational experiment. We would obtain many realizations of the estimator.

  • Fluctuations. Distributional information tells us the size and frequency of its fluctuations, complementing the statement that its error vanishes.

1. Weak convergence

◆ High priorityConvergence in distribution

Let \(X_n\)XnX_n and \(X\)XX be random vectors in a fixed dimension \(d\)dd, with joint CDFs \(F_n\)FnF_n and \(F\)FF. Definition 1

We say that \(F_n\)FnF_n converges weakly to \(F\)FF, written \(F_n\Rightarrow F\)Fn⇒FF_n\Rightarrow F or \(F_n\stackrel{w}{\longrightarrow}F\)Fn→wFF_n\stackrel{w}{\longrightarrow}F, if

Remark 1Terminology and notation

“Convergence in distribution,” “weak convergence,” and “convergence in law” describe the same notion for random vectors. Common notations are \[X_n\stackrel{d}{\longrightarrow}X,\qquad X_n\stackrel{D}{\longrightarrow}X,\qquad X_n\Rightarrow X.\]Xn→dX,Xn→DX,Xn⇒X.X_n\stackrel{d}{\longrightarrow}X,\qquad X_n\stackrel{D}{\longrightarrow}X,\qquad X_n\Rightarrow X. Statistics texts often use the first phrase. Weak convergence is also used for probability measures on spaces more general than \(\R^d\)ℝd\mathbb{R}^d. The symbol \(X\stackrel{d}{=}Y\)X=dYX\stackrel{d}{=}Y means equality of distributions, not pointwise equality of random variables.

◆ High priorityThe distribution matters, not the coupling

Remark 2

  • Probability spaces. Unlike convergence in probability or almost surely, convergence in distribution does not require \(X,X_1,X_2,\ldots\)X,X1,X2,…X,X_1,X_2,\ldots to live on the same probability space. It compares their induced probability measures.

  • Normal symmetry. For \(G\sim N(0,1)\)G∼N(0,1)G\sim N(0,1), symmetry gives \[X_n\Rightarrow G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X_n\Rightarrow -G, \qquad G\stackrel{d}{=}-G.\]Xn⇒G⇔Xn⇒−G,G=d−G.X_n\Rightarrow G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X_n\Rightarrow -G, \qquad G\stackrel{d}{=}-G.

Instructor’s NoteA distribution forgets the labels

Same pmf under shuffling. Imagine two columns of observations. If we shuffle one column, its histogram stays the same, but its row-by-row relationship with the other column changes. 1.1. Equivalent ways to check weak convergence

◆ High priorityTest functions and characteristic functions

Let \(C_b(\R^d)\)Cb(ℝd)C_b(\mathbb{R}^d) denote the bounded, continuous, real-valued functions on \(\R^d\)ℝd\mathbb{R}^d. A test function \(h\)hh probes a distribution through \(\E h(X)\)𝔼h(X)\mathbb{E}h(X). Boundedness ensures that this expectation exists without an additional moment assumption.

Characteristic functions.

The characteristic function of a random vector \(X\)XX is \[\varphi_X(t)=\E e^{it^\top X},\qquad t\in\R^d.\]φX(t)=𝔼eit⊤X,t∈ℝd.\varphi_X(t)=\mathbb{E}e^{it^\top X},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^d. 2. Central limit theorem

◆ High priorityThe Normal limit of a sample mean

For a random vector \(X=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d)^\top\)X=(ξ1,…,ξd)⊤X=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d)^\top, finite second moments ensure that all covariance entries exist. Indeed, \(\E|\xi_j\xi_k|\le(\E\xi_j^2\E\xi_k^2)^{1/2}\)𝔼|ξjξk|≤(𝔼ξj2𝔼ξk2)1/2\mathbb{E}|\xi_j\xi_k|\le(\mathbb{E}\xi_j^2\mathbb{E}\xi_k^2)^{1/2} by Cauchy–Schwarz. Theorem 3Central limit theorem

Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\)X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldots be i.i.d. random vectors in a fixed \(\R^d\)ℝd\mathbb{R}^d, with \(\E\|X_1\|^2<\infty\)𝔼∥X1∥2<∞\mathbb{E}\|X_1\|^2<\infty.   • Location. A law of large numbers locates the sample mean near \(\mu\)μ\mu. The CLT magnifies its error by \(\sqrt n\)n\sqrt n to retain a nontrivial limiting shape.

  • Scale. In dimension one, the unscaled average has variance \(\sigma^2/n\)σ2/n\sigma^2/n, whereas \(\sqrt n(\overline X_n-\mu)\)n(X¯n−μ)\sqrt n(\overline X_n-\mu) has variance \(\sigma^2\)σ2\sigma^2.

3. Three proof ideas for the CLT

◆ High priorityReduction to centered scalar variables

The Cramér–Wold device reduces the vector statement to scalar projections. For each fixed \(c\in\R^d\)c∈ℝdc\in\mathbb{R}^d, the required limit is \[\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n c^\top(X_i-\mu) \Rightarrow N(0,c^\top\Sigma c).\]1n∑i=1nc⊤(Xi−μ)⇒N(0,c⊤Σc).\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n c^\top(X_i-\mu) \Rightarrow N(0,c^\top\Sigma c).   • Scale invariance. The standardized third absolute moment \(\rho/\sigma^3\)ρ/σ3\rho/\sigma^3 is unchanged when the observations are multiplied by a nonzero constant. A bound without this normalization cannot hold uniformly over scales.

  • CDF error. The theorem implies the CLT under its additional third-moment assumption and supplies a uniform finite-sample CDF error bound. It need not be small for a particular \(n\)nn, and it gives no density approximation. See [1] (Section 3.4.4) for a proof.

  • Proof distinction.
